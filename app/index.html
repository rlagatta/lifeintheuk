UK Today, connected to the journey

United Kingdom

England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland. Great Britain means England, Scotland and Wales.

Four capitals

London, Edinburgh, Cardiff and Belfast. Dublin is the capital of the independent Republic of Ireland.

Identity and values

Democracy, rule of law, individual liberty, tolerance of different faiths and beliefs, and participation in community life.

UK Parliament
650 MPs

Commons and Lords at Westminster. The Speaker chairs Commons debates, is neutral, maintains order and is chosen by MPs in a secret ballot.

Scottish Parliament
129 MSPs

Meets at Holyrood in Edinburgh. Substantial devolved powers (handbook).

Northern Ireland Assembly
90 MLAs

Meets at Stormont in Belfast. Modern story tied to the 1998 Good Friday Agreement.

Senedd / Welsh Assembly
60 (handbook)

The study source uses the historic 60-member figure and older “Welsh Assembly” terminology. Verify current arrangements against official Senedd information.

Big Ben

Memory distinction: Big Ben is the great bell, not the whole Palace of Westminster (Elizabeth Tower).

Voting evolution

1832 and 1867 reforms expanded representation; suffragettes campaigned for women’s votes; equal voting age at 21 in 1928; voting age became 18 in 1969.

Before partition

Ireland had a complex relationship with English and later UK rule. Plantations in Ulster had lasting consequences.

1921–22 partition

Six northern counties remained in the UK as Northern Ireland; the rest became the Irish Free State and a republic in 1949.

Peace process

The 1998 Good Friday Agreement underpins the modern Northern Ireland settlement and devolution story.

Population table
67.6m (2022 est.)

Handbook series from just over 4m (1600) to estimated 67.6m (2022). England ~84%.

Identity and diversity

Ethnically diverse population, especially in large cities; origins from across the world. Nearly 10% have a parent or grandparent born outside the UK.

Religion (2009 survey)

Christian 70%, no religion 21%, Muslim 4%, Hindu 2%, Sikh 1%, Jewish & Buddhist each under 0.5%, other 2%.

Prizes

Mercury Prize, BRIT Awards, BAFTA, Olivier, Turner Prize, Booker.

Honours

Victoria Cross for acts of valour. Nobel laureates in the study story include Fleming, Crick and Kipling.

Landmarks

Stonehenge, Skara Brae, castles, cathedrals, Westminster and the Cenotaph link places to periods.

Transport

Steam power, railways, Brunel engineering, jet engine, hovercraft, Harrier and Concorde.

Information

Television, radar, Turing machine, ATM and World Wide Web.

Medicine and atomic science

Penicillin, insulin, DNA research, IVF, MRI and cloning. Rutherford’s atom research is linked in the handbook to later wartime atomic work.

Golf and tennis

St Andrews is known as the home of golf; Wimbledon is the famous London tennis championship.

Olympic anchors

Sir Steve Redgrave won rowing gold at five consecutive Games; Mary Peters won pentathlon gold in 1972 (strongly associated with Northern Ireland sport).

Minimum ages (study source)

16: moped and beer/wine with a meal when accompanied by someone 18+. 17: car/motorcycle. 18: buy alcohol, vote, stand for public office, jury service and listed adult activities.

Commonwealth

Developed as former colonies became independent. It has no power over members but can suspend membership.

NATO

The UK joined the post-war North Atlantic alliance.

Europe and Brexit

The UK joined the EEC in 1973, held an EU referendum in 2016, and formally left the EU on 31 January 2020.

UK Today · Demographics

Figures from Life in the UK: A guide for new residents, 3rd edition (population table, 2009 Citizenship Survey, nation shares).

Population growth (handbook series)

1600 just over 4m → 1801 8m → 1901 40m → 1951 50m → 2010 just over 62m → 2022 estimated 67.6m. Recent growth linked to migration and longer life expectancy.

Share by nation (handbook)

England ~84%, Scotland just over 8%, Wales around 5%, Northern Ireland under 3%.

Religion (2009 Citizenship Survey)

Christian 70%, no religion 21%, Muslim 4%, Hindu 2%, Sikh 1%, Jewish and Buddhist each under 0.5%, other 2%.

Key facts

  • Nearly 10% of the population has a parent or grandparent born outside the UK (handbook context).
  • English is the main language; Welsh, Gaelic and Irish Gaelic are also spoken in parts of the UK.
  • The UK is a constitutional monarchy with a parliamentary democracy.
